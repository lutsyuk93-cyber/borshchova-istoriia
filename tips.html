In practice

6 tips for homemade borscht

These tips complement the base recipe and help you get a more consistent result.

01

Skim the broth

Once it boils, remove the foam with a slotted spoon. The broth will be clearer and more appealing.

02

Don't overcook the cabbage

Add cabbage with its variety and your preferred texture in mind. It should stay tender, not mushy.

03

Work with the beet's color

Acid helps keep the color vibrant. The recipe uses a little lemon juice or vinegar for exactly that reason.

04

Don't rush the sauté

Onion, carrot, beet, and tomato paste need to cook through together without burning.

05

Add garlic at the end

Adding garlic after turning off the heat helps preserve its aroma.

06

Let it rest

After cooking, leave the dish covered for 15–20 minutes. Resting helps the flavors come together.

Storage

How to store and reheat borscht

Cool finished borscht and refrigerate it. For the best quality, reheat only the portion you need rather than the whole pot each time.

Does borscht taste better the next day?

After cooling and reheating, the flavors can become more pronounced. That's exactly why borscht is often made in a big batch.

How do I avoid losing flavor when reheating?

Reheat over moderate heat and don't let it boil longer than necessary. Add fresh herbs right before serving.
